Ligand Based Drug Design Tutorial : Using Known Actives to Discover New Drug Candidates

Not every drug discovery project has access to a high-quality protein structure. Ligand based drug design provides a powerful alternative by using the properties of known active compounds to guide the discovery of new ones. This ligand based drug design tutorial explains the key methods, tools, and workflows for applying ligand-based approaches to drug discovery, from pharmacophore modeling to QSAR and similarity-based virtual screening.


This ligand based drug design tutorial is suitable for researchers who have a set of known active compounds but lack a protein structure, or who want to complement structure-based methods with ligand-based approaches. By the end of this ligand based drug design tutorial, you will understand how to extract and apply the chemical information encoded in known actives to find new drug candidates.

What Is Ligand Based Drug Design?

Ligand based drug design is a drug discovery approach that uses the structural and physicochemical properties of known active compounds to guide the design and discovery of new molecules with similar or improved biological activity. Ligand based drug design does not require knowledge of the target protein structure, making it applicable when structural data is unavailable. The main methods in ligand based drug design include pharmacophore modeling, QSAR modeling, and molecular similarity searching.

  • Pharmacophore modeling from known active compounds
  • QSAR modeling for activity prediction
  • Molecular similarity searching for analog identification
  • Shape and electrostatic similarity-based virtual screening

Steps in This Ligand Based Drug Design Tutorial

This ligand based drug design tutorial follows these steps: first, curate a dataset of known active and inactive compounds with measured biological activity. Second, generate 3D conformations for all compounds. Third, build a pharmacophore model from the common features of active compounds. Fourth, build a QSAR model relating molecular descriptors to activity. Fifth, use the pharmacophore model and QSAR model to screen virtual compound libraries. Sixth, select and test the top-ranked virtual hits.

  • Step 1: Curate active and inactive compound datasets
  • Step 2: Generate 3D conformations for all compounds
  • Step 3: Build pharmacophore model from active compound features
  • Step 4: Build QSAR model for activity prediction
  • Step 5: Virtual screening with pharmacophore and QSAR models
  • Step 6: Select and experimentally test top virtual hits

Tools for This Ligand Based Drug Design Tutorial

This ligand based drug design tutorial recommends Phase or LigandScout for pharmacophore modeling, RDKit or Mordred for molecular descriptor calculation, scikit-learn for QSAR model building, and ROCS for shape-based similarity searching. For virtual library screening, ZINC and Enamine REAL provide large collections of commercially available compounds. This ligand based drug design tutorial uses freely available tools where possible to maximize accessibility.

  • Phase or LigandScout for pharmacophore modeling
  • RDKit and Mordred for descriptor calculation
  • Scikit-learn for QSAR model building
  • ROCS for shape-based similarity searching

Future of Ligand Based Drug Design

The future of ligand based drug design includes deep learning models that learn molecular representations directly from structure, generative AI that designs new molecules satisfying ligand-based constraints, and multi-modal models that combine ligand-based and structure-based information. As AI improves the accuracy of ligand-based predictions, this ligand based drug design tutorial approach will remain a valuable and widely applied drug discovery strategy.

  • Deep learning molecular representation learning
  • Generative AI for ligand-based constrained design
  • Multi-modal ligand and structure-based hybrid methods
  • Improved accuracy through larger training datasets
